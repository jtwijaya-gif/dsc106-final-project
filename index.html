Same Planet, Different Futures

Where Does Our Choice Matter Most?

How different could our planet look by 2100? Different emissions scenarios may lead to similar temperatures at first, but the differences become much clearer over time. Explore the interactive map and line chart to see how global temperatures could change under different emissions scenarios and which regions may experience the most warming.

Explore Future Warming

Use the sliders to explore different emissions scenarios and years, or press play to watch temperatures change over time. Hover over the map to see the temperature change at a specific location, or click anywhere on the map to compare how that location changes under different scenarios. You can also click the line chart to jump to a specific year.

What Can We Learn?

The visualizations show how different emissions scenarios can lead to very different temperature changes by 2100. Although the scenarios start with similar temperatures, they begin to separate more as time goes on. Under the high-emissions scenario, global temperatures could increase by around 3.7°C compared to the 1991–2010 average, while the low-emissions scenario shows an increase of around 0.9°C. The map also shows that warming is not the same everywhere, with some regions experiencing much larger changes than others.

Key Takeaway

The future of global warming depends heavily on the emissions scenario. While the differences may seem small at first, they become much larger toward 2100. By exploring different scenarios, years, and locations, we can see how different emissions paths could lead to very different futures for the same planet.

Project Progress

What have we done so far?

So far, our team has decided to focus on how different emissions scenarios could affect global temperatures by 2100. We used the CMIP6 climate dataset to build an interactive website with a global temperature map and a line chart. Users can select different emissions scenarios, change the year from 1900 to 2100, or play an animation to see how temperatures change over time. We also added a feature that lets users click on the map to compare temperature changes at a specific location. Our next step is to improve the design, test the interactions, and make the website easier to understand before the final submission.

What will be the most challenging part of your project to design, and why?

We think the most challenging part will be making sure the interactive map and line chart work well together while keeping everything easy to understand. Since our dataset covers many locations, years, and emissions scenarios, we need to make sure the visualizations update correctly when users change the controls. We also want to make sure the colors and labels clearly show temperature differences without making the website too complicated. Another challenge will be helping users understand why the differences between emissions scenarios become more noticeable toward the end of the century. Our goal is to make the website interactive and informative without overwhelming the reader.
